The Secret · Byron Preiss, 1982 · Casque 5 of 12

Chicago —
The First Casque

We at the Voynich Times spend a lot of our days on hunts that never ended. This one did — and fast. In 1982, Byron Preiss buried twelve casques in twelve North American cities, each holding a key that could be redeemed for a birthstone. Chicago's was the first ever found: three Chicago teenagers read a painting and Verse 12 as walking directions, followed them through Grant Park step by step, and dug the casque up in 1983, a year after the book appeared. We've walked that verse ourselves (well, on paper, at least) and it still stings that three kids beat us to it!

Section 2 · Verse 12

The Verse

Read it as walking directions: south along Michigan Avenue, east into Grant Park at Congress, then northeast to Lincoln and northwest into the grove.

Where M and B are set in stone And to Congress, R is known L sits and left Beyond his shoulder Is the Fair Folks' Treasure holder The end of ten by thirteen Is your clue Fence and fixture Central too For finding jewel casque Seek the sounds Of rumble Brush and music Hush.
VERSE 12 · FROM THE SECRET (1982)
“Where M and B are set in stone”

Mozart and Beethoven, engraved on Orchestra Hall (Symphony Center), 220 S. Michigan Ave. Preiss's own gloss — but disputed; the community alternative is “Man and Beast” (the Bowman and his horse). Both readings put you at the same corner. See Disputes.

“And to Congress, R is known”

R = Roosevelt: Roosevelt University's Auditorium Building, Michigan Ave at Congress (now Ida B. Wells Dr).

“L sits and left / Beyond his shoulder”

L = Lincoln: Saint-Gaudens' Seated Lincoln, Grant Park. Approached from the Bowman, you face him looking north; left past his shoulder aims you northwest into the grove.

“Is the Fair Folks' / Treasure holder”

The Art Institute of Chicago — built for the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition, holder of treasures, one block west.

“The end of ten by thirteen / Is your clue”

Two planted tree lines — one of ten, one of thirteen — crossing in the grove; the casque at the end/intersection. By 1983 the city had cut a tree from each row, breaking the count until Preiss told the finders to adjust for missing trees. Orientation disputed — see Disputes.

“Fence and fixture / Central too”

The arched fence (drawn in the painting) + an electrical fixture/box on the bridge wall + Central = the Illinois Central tracks beside the site.

“Seek the sounds / Of rumble”

Trains on the IC tracks, directly beside and under the Jackson bridge.

“Brush and music”

Brush = the Art Institute (paint). Music = the Petrillo Band Shell in Grant Park.

“Hush.”

Unresolved Best community guess: the digging was in a very public place.

Section 4 · 1983

The Dig

The solve and the recovery were two different fights. The boys had the right park and the right corner relatively early; the tree grid nearly beat them.

1 · The early digs

Working from the painting and verse, Gasiorowski, Wrobel, and James dug roughly a half-dozen holes across Grant Park without success.

2 · They wrote to Preiss

The finders sent Preiss photographs of where they'd been looking. He told them they were on the right track — and sent back a photograph of the precise burial location, the freshly patted earth as he'd left it. (Preiss's photo-assist was a one-time product of 1983; the rules of engagement changed after Chicago.)

3 · The tree call

Wrobel got Preiss on the phone and walked him through their process. At the end Preiss asked what they made of “10 × 13.” The counts didn't add up — not 10 in one row, not 13 in the other. Preiss “chuckled” and told them to recount and adjust for any trees that might be missing. The city had cut down one tree in each row — with the gaps counted as positions, the lines suddenly lined up.

4 · The final hole

Even lined up, the big final hole missed: the box was further to the side than they thought. Frustrated, one of them threw a shovel into the hole; dirt slid off the side wall and exposed the Plexiglas box, its top only about 12 inches below the surface.

5 · The redemption

Inside the ceramic casque was a small key. Mailed to Preiss, it was exchanged for the emerald. The finders kept the hand-painted ceramic casque itself, valued at $500 in 1983.

“Without the information made available to us, the site would never have been found due to the change in the count of the trees. We could have finished the hunt almost a year earlier.”

— ERIC GASIOROWSKI, FINDER'S WRITE-UP [4]

Who was actually at the dig

The credited finders are Eric Gasiorowski, Rob Wrobel, and David James, and the three were to split the find three ways. The community record adds two corrections worth keeping attached to the credit:

· David James was not present on the day of the discovery, though he is counted a finder.

· Danny Rosenbach, a friend of the group, is the one who physically discovered/uncovered the casque in the hole (per the Quest4Treasure reading of the Tribune account).

Section 6 · Kept honest

Disputes & Open Questions

Where the sources conflict, both readings are carried and the conflict is named — not smoothed over. The site today: the trees are gone (the east–west line is 80% cut; only 3 of the original 13 remain in the north–south line), and the fencepost at the spot no longer carries its arched “halo” section. The solve is a historical reconstruction, not re-walkable end-to-end.

Uncertainty · ±50 m

Where exactly was it?

Two coordinates stand in the record, ~37 m apart, both inside the finders' own spread of recollections. Gasiorowski's marker photo puts the spot much closer to the Jackson wall than the Street View panorama's apparent casque/replica. This dossier keeps both numbers visible rather than pretending to a precision the record doesn't have.

Disputed

“M and B” — Mozart & Beethoven vs. Man and Beast

The wiki relays Preiss's own answer (Mozart and Beethoven, carved on Orchestra Hall). The Quest4Treasure old guard insists M and B stands for Man and Beast — the Bowman (man) and his horse (beast) — “not Mozart and Beethoven as Mr. Preiss has said.” Both readings put you at the same corner of Michigan Ave, which is why the solve survives the dispute. Status: unresolved, low-stakes.

Disputed

Which row was 10, and which was 13?

Gasiorowski's write-up says 10 east–west, 13 north–south. The kids' own hand-drawn map (glimpsed in the 2018 ABC7 footage) labels the east–west dots “13” and the north–south dots “10” — the reverse. It may be fallible memory, or green-ink labels added later by an unknown hand. Status: unresolved, and now unverifiable — the trees are gone.

Open

Exact find date

Only the year (1983) and the Tribune's August 9, 1983 coverage are firm. Some publisher reissue blurbs say 1984 — treated as publisher error.

Open

“Hush.”

No agreed reading. Best community guess: the digging was in a very public place.

Open

Collar numerals & the ear-flap symbol

The “2” and “88” on the painting's collar, and the symbol at the hat's ear flap, remain unassigned.

Field notes

If visiting: the site is public parkland beside an active rail corridor — view from the Jackson Dr sidewalk / Street View side; the tracks are Metra Electric / South Shore, live and fenced. Nothing to dig: the casque is recovered, and the tree grid that proved the spot no longer exists. The single best pre-visit artifact is Gasiorowski's marker photo (his copy of the book on the ground at the dig spot, facing west toward the fence and tracks), hosted on the wiki solution page [1].
