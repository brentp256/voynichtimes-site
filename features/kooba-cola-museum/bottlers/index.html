The Bottlers

Kooba was advertised nationally and bottled locally — a handful of real firms, most of them visible only in a launch ad, a want ad, or a single line of newsprint. These are the ones the record names. Every fact below comes from the museum's catalogued sources; each entry links to them.

The Kooba Corporation — New York

The principal. Victor S. Fox's own company, owner of the trademark and author of the campaign.

Offices at 247 Park Avenue, New York — expanded in January 1941, when the corporation and its Fox affiliates leased the eighth floor there, supplementing the ninth floor of the adjoining Grand Central Palace at 480 Lexington Avenue (KCM-194). Cap redemptions were mailed to 125 East 46th Street; the telephone was PLaza 8-0100. The corporation's own announcement in the Brooklyn Daily Eagle (March 17, 1941) adds its plant: Kooba "bottled fresh daily at the new Brooklyn plant at 705 Driggs Ave.," with dealer, wholesaler and jobber recruitment run from the main office (KCM-195, Gallery Room VIII). No separate Brooklyn operating company is named in the ad — the plant was the corporation's own.

Malolo Soda Works — Honolulu, Hawaii

The bottler who ran the whole campaign, exactly as designed, for one winter.

1340 N. School Street, Honolulu. Proprietor Chang Chow; manager Alex Y. Leong; a firm then in business for 15 years. Malolo launched Kooba in Hawaii on December 16, 1940 — in the large 12-ounce bottle only, at 5¢ — with launch articles in both Honolulu dailies, a sentence-completion contest ($15 in cash plus 20 cases; entries with one Kooba cap or facsimile, due December 24), and a premium catalog at its dealers and factory. Its telephone number was printed wrong in the launch ad — 4149 for 8149 — and corrected in both papers within two days. The contest drew a "hearty response" and more than 200 entries; the winners were announced January 12, 1941. The Honolulu pages are catalogued as KCM-181–KCM-191 and hang in Gallery Room VII. The papers say "Distributed by Malolo Soda Works" and that in Honolulu Kooba was bottled only in the 12-ounce bottle; whether Malolo bottled on the premises at N. School Street the record does not say, and the museum does not guess.

Kooba Bottling Company of Miami, Inc. — Miami, Florida

Fox's own bottling company — and the first place the end shows.

Chartered November 18, 1940, with Victor S. Fox himself listed as a director. The plant bottled Kooba at 1333 NE Bayshore Place through 1941 — the address survives in the museum's record because a 1965 Miami Herald columnist reconstructed it from a found coupon: "Kooba was put out by the Kooba Bottling Co. of Miami, 1333 NE Bayshore Pl." (KCM-192, Gallery Room IX). Liquidation ads ran in December 1941; the last traces fade in the spring of 1942; the company was dissolved in 1945. The plant's site was later occupied by the Miami Herald's own building.

Fresco Beverage Bottling Company — Long Island City, Queens

Kooba's Long Island City bottler, visible in its hiring ads.

21-08 35th Road, Long Island City. Fresco's want ad in the Long Island Star-Journal of May 20, 1941 — "SALESMAN WANTED, sell new sensational Kooba Cola, salary and commission" — is catalogued twice over: as the web-entered record KCM-094 and as the newspaper page itself, KCM-200. Fresco's Kooba want ads also appear in the David Saunders biography of Victor Fox (KCM-085).

The distributor frontier — Long Island & Queens, spring 1941

Not bottlers — the recruitment layer the comics' circulation was supposed to feed.

In April and May 1941 the want ads multiply: salesmen and distributors wanted for the Nassau–Suffolk territory, replies to Box 141, Lindenhurst, or phone Amityville 1751 (KCM-196); an individual distributor in Astoria, N. Di Nero, recruiting his own salesmen to sell the "new sensational Kooba-Oola" drink (KCM-197); and in Jamaica, distributors with trucks wanted at 180-05 Linden Blvd. (KCM-201). None of these advertisers is named as a bottler, and the museum lists them as what the pages show: men with territories, trucks, or neither, answering the campaign. Whether any of them ever sold a bottle, the record does not say.
