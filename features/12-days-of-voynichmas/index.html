The 12 Days of Voynichmas

Twelve picks for the season, all of them things you solve. The calendars come first because a calendar bought on December 3rd is a calendar already behind. The books carry the rest of December.

Day One — EXIT Advent Calendar: The Missing Hollywood Star

Thames & Kosmos (2024)

We played this one. It went through our house last December, a door a day: a Christmas movie shooting in Hollywood, the star missing, and 24 days of riddles between you and the premiere. It is the strongest seller of this year's EXIT calendars (3K+ bought in the past month, 4.6 stars across 623 ratings), and having played it, we get it.

The catch is the EXIT format itself. You cut and fold and write on the pieces as you go, so it plays exactly once. That is the deal with every EXIT game. This one is worth its one play.

Day Two — EXIT Advent Calendar: The Intergalactic Race

Thames & Kosmos (2025)

Santa's sleigh, entered in a race across the galaxy. The newest EXIT calendar takes the same 24 doors off Earth (1K+ bought in the past month, and the same EXIT bargain as Day One: it plays once).

The rating is 4.1 stars across 371, and the complaints are about the doors. They are small and perforated and they do not always tear the way they should. The puzzles themselves are fine. Know about the doors going in, and the race is worth running.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Three — Hidden Games: Professor Charlie's World Tour

Hidden Games

Professor Charlie is taking a trip around the world, and the calendar follows him, a puzzle a day. It is the English edition of a German original from 2021, and it already sits in this paper's own catalog (entry 69), which is where we first met it.

Checked this week, it is in stock, whatever the older notes on it said. One envelope a day is the design. Tearing ahead is between you and your conscience.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Four — Mystery Escape Room Advent Calendar: Bramblewood Cabin

Falkner & Riddlehaus

A book, not a box. Twenty-four secrets in a snowed-in cabin, a hundred pages and more of logic puzzles and visual puzzles, with hints in two steps (a nudge first, the answer only if the nudge fails). A former guest left coded messages behind, somebody never checked out, and the last puzzle lands on Christmas Eve.

It sits in our catalog too (entry 87). A book travels better than a cardboard calendar does, and this one plays alone or handed around a table.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Five — Merry Murdle

G. T. Karber · St. Martin's Griffin

Twenty-five festive logic puzzles. Santa is missing, Deductive Logico is on the case, and the grid does not lie. Karber's Murdle books are the current form of a very old pleasure (he wrote the introduction to the new edition of The Baffle Book, so the line from 1928 to this stocking is a short one).

A paperback and a pencil. It fits a stocking, and it does not expire on December 1st.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Six — The Minute Between Alibis

Eliza Northwick

A murder mystery dealt out over 24 days, one connected case, an installment at a time. The title is the shape of it: every alibi has a minute in it that does not line up, and the case hides in that minute.

We know less about this one than the rest of the twelve. It is small-press, it is new, and the record on it is thin. It is here on the strength of the form, a Christmas murder told a day at a time, which the big houses have mostly quit making.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Seven — Clue: Secrets Under Snowfall

Hasbro

The second one we played ourselves. The calendar opens like a storybook, a map of Tudor Mansion on one side and a crime board on the other, and 22 daily puzzles build to one case: who killed Boddy Black at his winter gala, and where, and with what. A cipher does the code work. It plays once (you fold, cut, and write on some of it), the mystery is family-weight rather than a brain-burner, and it is rated 10 and up.

Then there are the tokens. Six of them, one for each suspect, cast in clear blue, the characters in their winter clothes. They are beautiful things, and they outlive the calendar: Hasbro made them to drop straight into a regular game of Clue as your pawns when December is over. The mystery is the excuse to open the doors. The tokens are what you keep.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Eight — The Midnight Choir Murders

Arthur J. Clockwell

A cathedral choir at Christmas, a murder, and 24 puzzles to solve it with. That is most of what we know: author, title, and form. It is self-published, and the production is plain.

It is here because a choir at midnight is the right setting for a Christmas murder, and nobody else in the twelve used it.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Nine — The Christmas Eve Murder of 1926

Harry Lezling

Christmas Eve, 1926. A murder, and an escape-room advent calendar to work your way through it, a puzzle a day. It is the period piece of the twelve, set in the decade when the fair-play mystery was young.

It is also the newest thing here (published this past September), which means the record on it is thin. New and thin is the whole of the warning.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Ten — The Haunted Escape Room Advent Calendar

Silas Holloway

A haunted house at Christmas, and a ghost's curse to break over 24 days of escape-room puzzles. The ghost story is the oldest Christmas tradition there is, and this is the only pick that makes it playable.

Now the record. The ratings on this one are bad. Not soft, bad (checked this week). If you want a polished solve, Days One through Four are up the page and your money is better spent there. This one is for the buyer who collects haunted-house anything and forgives a rough edge. That buyer exists. This listing is for them, with the record showing.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Eleven — Time Travelers to Save Christmas

Arthur J. Clockwell

Clockwell again (his second slot in the twelve, and yes, we noticed). As Guardian of Time you travel to the moments that shaped the holiday and repair a timeline somebody has been sabotaging, a puzzle a day, before Christmas comes undone.

It is a slim book, 94 pages, with hints in three levels, so a family can play it without the youngest solver stalling the whole table.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Twelve — The Christmas Murder Game

Alexandra Benedict · Poisoned Pen Press

The closer. A country-house Christmas mystery that hands the solving to the reader: twelve clues and twelve keys across the twelve days of Christmas, anagrams of the carol's gifts worked into the text, and twelve mystery titles hidden in it for good measure. It takes the last day. Twelve days, twelve keys. The shape of the book is the shape of the season.

The rating is 3.8 stars across some 3,900 ratings, soft for a closer, and it is printed here because it is true. Puzzle novels split their readers. The ones who wanted a puzzle got one.
