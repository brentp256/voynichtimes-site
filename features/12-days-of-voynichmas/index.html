The 12 Days of Voynichmas

December is the one month this paper's whole territory turns into gifts. What follows is twelve of them, all Christmas, all things you solve: escape-room calendars, puzzle calendars, and murder mysteries that hand the case to the reader.

The calendars come first, because a calendar bought on December 3rd is a calendar already behind. The books carry the rest of the month.

Day One — EXIT Advent Calendar: The Missing Hollywood Star

Thames & Kosmos (2024)

We open with the one we can vouch for in person. This calendar went through our house last December, one door a day: a Christmas movie being shot in Hollywood, a star gone missing, and 24 days of riddles between you and the premiere. It is the strongest seller of this year's EXIT calendars (3K+ bought in the past month, 4.6 stars across 623 ratings), and having played it, we get it. The set pieces give the puzzles somewhere to live.

The complication is the EXIT format itself. You cut, fold, and mark the components as you go, so the calendar plays exactly once. That is the bargain with every game in this line. This one holds up its end.

Day Two — EXIT Advent Calendar: The Intergalactic Race

Thames & Kosmos (2025)

Santa's sleigh, entered in a race across the galaxy. The newest EXIT calendar takes the same 24 doors off-planet, and 1K+ buyers picked it up in the past month.

Fair listing, so here is the whole of it. The rating sits at 4.1 stars across 371 ratings, and the complaints waiting there are about the doors (small perforated doors, and they do not always tear the way they should). We are listing it anyway. The race is a good frame and the puzzles carry it. Just know the doors fight back a little.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Three — Hidden Games: Professor Charlie's World Tour

Hidden Games

A puzzle advent calendar with a case to work: Professor Charlie's trip around the world, one day and one puzzle at a time. It is the English edition of a German original from 2021, and it already lives in this paper's own catalog (entry 69 in the database), which is where we first met it.

Checked this week: in stock, whatever older notes said. The format asks a little patience. One envelope a day is the design (tearing ahead is between you and your conscience).

Buy on Amazon →

Day Four — Mystery Escape Room Advent Calendar: Bramblewood Cabin

Falkner & Riddlehaus

Twenty-four secrets in a snowed-in cabin, in a book of a hundred pages and more: logic puzzles and visual puzzles, with hints given in two steps: a nudge first, the answer only if the nudge fails. It sits in our catalog too (entry 87).

Know what you are buying. This is a book, not a box of doors. The cabin is a story you read and solve your way through, which suits some houses better than cardboard does.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Five — Merry Murdle

G. T. Karber · St. Martin's Griffin

Twenty-five festive murder-mystery logic puzzles. Santa is missing, Deductive Logico is on the case, and the grid does not lie. Karber's Murdle books are the modern form of the logic-grid mystery this paper has been reading about in 1928 (his introduction opens the new edition of The Baffle Book, in fact).

It fits a stocking, which is not nothing. A paperback, a pencil, one detective at a time. Solo work, not a party game (the person who does the crossword in ink will understand).

Buy on Amazon →

Day Six — The Minute Between Alibis

Eliza Northwick

Every alibi has a minute in it somewhere. The minute nobody can account for is where the case hides, and Northwick hangs a Christmas murder on that minute. The title is the method: line the stories up and find the gap.

A small-press Christmas mystery, the kind the big houses have mostly stopped publishing. That is part of why it is here.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Seven — Clue: Secrets Under Snowfall

Hasbro

The second firsthand pick. We played this one too, and we will tell you the real reason it made the list: the doors hold little collectible pawns, and they are wonderful — small, weighty, worth the calendar by themselves to the kind of reader this paper has.

Fairly stated, it is Clue, so the mystery itself is family-weight, not a brain-burner. Buy it for the pawns and the daily ritual. Let the solving be the excuse.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Eight — The Midnight Choir Murders

Arthur J. Clockwell

Carolers, midnight, and a body. Clockwell takes the most peaceful sound of the season and puts a murder in the middle of it. A small-press puzzle mystery, from the end of the trade where Christmas mysteries you solve yourself are still being written.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Nine — The Christmas Eve Murder of 1926

Harry Lezling

A century back, to the golden age's own Christmas Eve. Lezling's 1926 murder is the period piece of the twelve, from the decade when the fair-play mystery was being invented in public. Some of us like our Christmas murders with a hundred years on them.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Ten — The Haunted Escape Room Advent Calendar

Silas Holloway

Now the listing we owe you the most honesty on. This haunted-house advent calendar's ratings are bad — checked this week, and bad is the word. If you want a polished, difficult, well-made solve, skip this one and spend the money on Days One through Four.

Why is it here at all? Because a haunted house at Christmas is a rare bird (the season's calendars run cozy, not creepy), and the buyer who collects haunted-house anything and forgives a rough edge exists. This one is for that buyer. Listed with its record showing.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Eleven — Time Travelers to Save Christmas

Arthur J. Clockwell

Clockwell's second appearance in these twelve days, and it is deliberate. When a small press carries two slots in a guide like this, that is the guide doing its job. This one is the romp of the list: time travelers, Christmas in danger, and a plot that treats the season as something worth rescuing properly.

Buy on Amazon →

Day Twelve — The Christmas Murder Game

Alexandra Benedict · Poisoned Pen Press

The closer, and the reason the guide is shaped the way it is. A country-house Christmas mystery that is itself a puzzle: twelve clues and twelve keys worked across the twelve days of Christmas, anagrams of the carol's gifts hidden in the text, and twelve more mystery titles tucked away for the reader to hunt. A mystery that hands the solving to you is this guide's whole idea, in one book. It takes the last day.

The fair note, because every listing here carries one. It stands at 3.8 stars across some 3,900 ratings, a soft number for a closer. Puzzle-forward novels split their readers, and the rating shows the split. The readers on the right side of it got the book they wanted.
