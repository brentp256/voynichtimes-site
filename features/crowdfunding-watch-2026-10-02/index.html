Crowdfunding Watch · Week of September 28–October 2, 2026

Crowdfunding Watch: Doomensions Returns storms Kickstarter as Blackwater Shadows closes unsolved

The week’s crowdfunding movers on the mystery beat — who’s soaring, who’s on the knife’s edge, and who never found their people.

Crowdfunding is where this hobby lives or dies. Every week, new puzzle boxes, case files, and escape-room games bet their existence on finding an audience in 30 days or less. This week: a pop-up haunted mansion at CA$286,000 and still climbing, a detective game that funded in three hours after its first campaign was cancelled, a 1,000-piece jigsaw fighting for its life at 36%, and a Tasmanian cold case that closed with sixteen backers and total silence. Here’s what moved on the mystery beat.

The week’s giant: Doomensions Returns

The Mysterious Package Company is back in the pop-up mansion business, and the crowd showed up fast. Doomensions Returns — two brand-new standalone experiences, Crimson Castle and Cursed Tomb — launched September 21 and is sitting at CA$285,925 against a CA$40,000 goal (about 715%) with 1,319 backers and three weeks still to run. The average pledge is a hefty CA$217, and the campaign is still adding roughly CA$6,400 and 30+ backers a day — this one is compounding, not coasting. For context, the original Doomensions: Pop-Up Mystery Manor (2022) pulled in CA$681,766 from 5,338 backers; at its current daily pace, the sequel has the low CA$400,000s in sight. If you missed the first haunted house, late pledges for the original opened via BackerKit alongside the new campaign. (Figures tracked by Tabletop Analytics, as of this writing.)

Status: Live — CA$285,925 of CA$40,000 (~715%), 1,319 backers. Ends October 22. Back it on Kickstarter →

Fresh on the board: The Witching Hour

PostCurious doesn’t launch campaigns so much as detonate them. The Witching Hour: A Puzzletale — a five-to-eight-hour boxed puzzle mystery set in the witch-haunted village of Old Miring, and the tabletop debut of BBC Radio comedy writer Rebecca Channon — launched September 29 and is already at $126,330 of a $15,000 goal (842%) with 1,760 backers. The average pledge sits at $72 against a $59 base game (it’ll retail at $65) — backers are loading up on add-ons and earlier titles. The press piled on fast: Room Escape Artist played it over two sittings (about six hours) and praised the story-puzzle blend; ARGNet called it a possible new favorite PostCurious game; it’s a Kickstarter “Project We Love” in the platform’s Witchstarter October showcase. And PostCurious is running weekly Friday puzzles for backers through the campaign, with a free enamel pin for anyone who completes the set — community engineering that keeps the comments warm for all 30 days. This is creator Rita Orlov’s eighth campaign; her previous seven raised over $1.6M from nearly 25,000 backers. (Figures tracked by Tabletop Analytics, as of this writing.)

Status: Live — $126,330 of $15,000 (842%), 1,760 backers. Ends October 29. Room Escape Artist review → · ARGNet’s take → · Live funding tracker →

Ending soon: A Question of Dragons

Red Herring Games’ A Question of Dragons — a cold-case mystery set in 999AD, where you sift a prior’s thousand-year-old investigation notes to decide whether Brother Osric was murdered or eaten by a dragon — is in its final stretch at 158% funded, with the campaign ending October 8. (Absolute pledged and backer figures weren’t visible at press time; the percentage comes from Kickstarter’s own project listings, as of ~September 27.) Designed for serious sleuths and cipher lovers — the trailer leans hard into marginalia decryption and the sainthood-or-dragon verdict.

Status: Funded — 158% with days to go. Ends October 8. Watch the trailer →

Also live: Journey to the Emerald City — and the crowd is arguing about it

The Oliver Twins’ Journey to the Emerald City — a storybook adventure with a cipher disc, a sealed double-sided Oz map, and a 52-card adventure deck — launched September 29 with a £5,000 goal and pulled in £1,500+ in the opening hours. Tiers run £18 (early bird, capped at 200) to a £100 six-copy bundle, with signed and special Dizzy editions capped at 100 and 200. But the crowd response has split down the middle: Time Extension torched the campaign’s AI-generated artwork, while Console Classics judged the presentation more careful than the twins’ earlier efforts but flagged unfinished visuals — and noted the long shadow of Dizzy Returns, the duo’s failed 2012 campaign. A comeback story or a repeat? The funding bar is modest; the credibility bar is the real boss fight. (As of this writing.)

Status: Live — £1,500+ of £5,000 in the opening hours. Back it on Kickstarter → · Console Classics’ measured take →

The one to watch: Encrypted Pieces

Ricardo Leidenz’s Encrypted Pieces Puzzles — a 1,000-piece narrative jigsaw where the finished art hides a cipher that opens a physical lock — is the week’s knife’s-edge: 36% funded with about 18 days left (per Kickstarter’s listings, as of ~September 27). Here’s the math that makes it interesting: 36% in roughly 12 days is 3% a day, and it needs about 3.5% a day from here. That’s barely above its current pace — and crowdfunding’s famous final-72-hour surge hasn’t kicked in yet. If the puzzle community rallies the way it did for Nightmere below, this one is savable. If the middle sags, it’s Blackwater territory. Watch this space.

Status: Live — ~36% funded with ~18 days remaining (as of this writing).

The quirky one: eXcape Room for Microsoft Excel

Yes, really — an escape room adventure built inside Microsoft Excel. Nandy & Woody’s spreadsheet puzzler has been climbing all week, from 114% to roughly 180% funded with about 20 days left (per Kickstarter’s project listings across recent crawls). The hook is genuinely clever: alongside the standard edition there’s a Stealth Edition disguised as a boring quarterly financial report, so you can play at your desk completely undetected. Zero installation, a free starter demo, and proof that this audience will follow a good puzzle anywhere — even into a pivot table.

Status: Live — ~180% funded, ~20 days left (as of this writing). Try the free demo →

The comeback: Nightmere I

The week’s best story isn’t just a funding number — it’s a resurrection. Cupkisu Studios’ Nightmere I, a hand-drawn psychological horror detective game, funded its CA$15,000 goal in three hours and sits around 229% (roughly CA$34,000) with the campaign running to October 27. But this is the second attempt: the original “Nightmere: Act I” campaign ran June 26–August 25 and was cancelled by the creator at CA$18,357 of a CA$35,350 goal (51%, 269 backers). The team cut the goal by more than half, rebuilt, and the audience came back. In a GAMERamble interview, the three-person team talked through the lessons from the cancellation — and drew a hard line against generative AI replacing artists: every sprite, background, cutscene, and UI element is hand-drawn. The fuel behind the relaunch: a playable demo with thousands of Steam wishlists, an enthusiastic itch.io comment section, and an audience of 290,000+ followers. Stretch goals now cover added localizations, animated cutscenes, expanded story content, and console ports for PlayStation, Xbox, and Switch. Entry starts at CA$25.

Status: Live — CA$15,000 goal funded in 3 hours, ~229%. Ends October 27. GAMERamble interview → · GoNintendo on the stretch goals → · Play the demo →

The cautionary tale: Blackwater Shadows

Not every campaign finds its audience. A Devil of a Mystery’s debut case file — a 2½-hour multimodal Tasmanian cold case from crime novelist and serving police officer Simon James Thiessen — ended September 23 with 16 backers and $2,952 AUD against an $8,000 goal, and not a single public comment on the campaign page. The few who found it believed in it — backers averaged about $185 AUD each. In his September 5 update, Thiessen wrote that “building an audience for an interactive mystery is definitely a different challenge than writing one.” Post-campaign updates titled “What’s next with Blackwater Shadows” suggest the detective isn’t done yet.

Status: Unsuccessful. Campaign page remains up. Campaign page →

Why it matters

The gap between Nightmere’s three-hour funding and Blackwater’s silent campaign page is the whole story of this niche — but this week’s numbers sharpen it. Doomensions can mobilize 1,319 backers at CA$217 a head on name recognition alone; a first-timer from Hobart with a genuinely interesting cold case couldn’t get sixteen people in a room. And Nightmere proves the audience can be rebuilt: cancel at 51%, cut the goal, come back, fund in three hours. The puzzles are rarely the problem — the audience is. That’s why we cover the failures alongside the wins. If you backed something this week, tell a friend who likes puzzles. That, more than any stretch goal, is what keeps the lights on.
