Crowdfunding Watch · Week of September 28–October 2, 2026

Crowdfunding Watch: Doomensions Returns storms Kickstarter as Blackwater Shadows closes unsolved

The week’s crowdfunding movers on the mystery beat — who’s soaring, who’s on the knife’s edge, and who never found their people.

Crowdfunding is where this hobby lives or dies. Every week, new puzzle boxes, case files, and escape-room games bet their existence on finding an audience in 30 days or less. Some storm past their goals in hours. Some never find their people at all. Here’s what moved this week on the mystery beat.

The week’s giant: Doomensions Returns

The Mysterious Package Company is back in the pop-up mansion business. Doomensions Returns — two brand-new standalone experiences, Crimson Castle and Cursed Tomb — launched September 22 and runs to October 22. The original Doomensions: Pop-Up Mystery Manor (2022) pulled in CA$681,766 from 5,338 backers; the sequel was already deep into six figures within its first day. If you missed the first haunted house, late pledges for the original opened via BackerKit alongside the new campaign.

Status: Live, funded many times over. Ends October 22. Back it on Kickstarter →

Fresh on the board: The Witching Hour

PostCurious launched The Witching Hour: A Puzzletale on September 29 — a five-to-eight-hour boxed puzzle mystery set in the witch-haunted village of Old Miring, and the tabletop debut of BBC Radio comedy writer Rebecca Channon. It’s a Kickstarter “Project We Love” and part of Witchstarter, the platform’s October supernatural showcase. Room Escape Artist has already played it and came away impressed with the story integration.

Status: Just launched (September 29). Live now on Kickstarter. Read the Room Escape Artist review →

Ending soon: A Question of Dragons

Red Herring Games’ A Question of Dragons — a cold-case mystery set in 999AD, where you sift a prior’s thousand-year-old investigation notes to decide whether Brother Osric was murdered or eaten by a dragon — was sitting at 158% funded with under two weeks left. The campaign ends October 8. Designed for serious sleuths and cipher lovers.

Status: Funded, final days. Ends October 8. Watch the trailer →

Also live: Journey to the Emerald City

The Oliver Twins’ Journey to the Emerald City — a storybook adventure with a cipher disc, a sealed double-sided Oz map, and a 52-card adventure deck — is mid-campaign on Kickstarter after a strong first day (£1,500+ against a £5,000 goal in the opening hours).

Status: Live. Back it on Kickstarter →

The one to watch: Encrypted Pieces

Ricardo Leidenz’s Encrypted Pieces Puzzles — a 1,000-piece narrative jigsaw where the finished art hides a cipher that opens a physical lock — was at 36% funded with roughly two and a half weeks left. This is the classic crowdfunding knife’s-edge: a clever concept that hasn’t found its crowd yet. Worth watching whether the puzzle community rallies in the final stretch.

Status: Live, ~36% funded, ~18 days remaining (as of this writing).

The quirky one: eXcape Room for Microsoft Excel

Yes, really — an escape room adventure built inside Microsoft Excel. Nandy & Woody’s spreadsheet puzzler was at 151% funded with about four weeks left. Proof that this audience will follow a good puzzle anywhere, even into a pivot table.

Status: Live, funded.

The digital outlier: Nightmere I

Not cardboard, but worth a mention: Cupkisu’s Nightmere I, a hand-drawn psychological horror detective game, funded its CA$15,000 goal in three hours and sits at 228%. You play a private investigator whose evidence and deductions double as combat against suspects’ lies. The campaign runs to October 27, with a playable demo on Steam.

Status: Live, funded. Ends October 27. Read more →

Recently funded: Disaster On Dino Island

The Detective Society’s dinosaur-sized mystery closed out September with £31,669 from 448 backers. Danger, deception, and dinosaurs — the award-winning team keeps delivering.

Status: Funded. (Late pledges via the campaign page.) Campaign page →

The cautionary tale: Blackwater Shadows

Not every campaign finds its audience. A Devil of a Mystery’s debut case file — a 2½-hour multimodal Tasmanian cold case from crime novelist and serving police officer Simon James Thiessen — ended September 23 with 16 backers and $2,952 AUD against an $8,000 goal, and not a single public comment on the campaign page. The few who found it believed in it — backers averaged about $185 AUD each. In his September 5 update, Thiessen wrote that “building an audience for an interactive mystery is definitely a different challenge than writing one.” Post-campaign updates titled “What’s next with Blackwater Shadows” suggest the detective isn’t done yet.

Status: Unsuccessful. Campaign page remains up. Campaign page →

Why it matters

The gap between Nightmere’s three-hour funding and Blackwater’s silent campaign page is the whole story of this niche: the puzzles are rarely the problem — the audience is. Doomensions can mobilize thousands of backers on name recognition alone; a first-timer from Hobart with a genuinely interesting cold case couldn’t get sixteen people in a room. That’s why we cover the failures alongside the wins. If you backed something this week, tell a friend who likes puzzles. That, more than any stretch goal, is what keeps the lights on.
