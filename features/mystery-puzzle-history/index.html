Mystery Puzzle History · Series Overview

The Early Golden Age

Our Mystery Puzzle History series traces where the hobby came from. It covers the earliest era, which we call The Early Golden Age: many of these works are not fully realised Mystery Puzzle Games, but they are the steps toward them.

The articles, in time order

  • 1843 — Mystery Puzzle Games and Their Origin From… Poe?
  • February 1928 — Dead on the Vine: Cooper's 1928 Cipher Stories
  • November 1928 — A Baffle Book Doth Make Sherlocks of Us All

Timeline

  1. 1843 — The Gold-Bug (cipher in a story)
  2. 1881 — The Cryptogram, book 2 of Eight Hundred Leagues on the Amazon by Jules Verne (descendant of The Gold-Bug)
  3. 1907 (June 30) — New York Tribune detective puzzle (Possibly the first…)
  4. 1926 — The Haverfordian publishes John Dickson Carr's The Shadow of the Goat, a short story that lets the reader try to solve the mystery before the next month's answer (We argue it is the first Mystery Puzzle Game)
  5. 1928 — February 28: Cipher Stories Puzzle Book — twenty-five original short stories, each containing at least two vital cipher puzzles
  6. 1928 — November: The Baffle Book published (Wren, McKay)
  7. 1929 — October: The 2nd Baffle Book published (Wren, McKay)
  8. 1930 — November 21: The Third Baffle Book published (Wren, McKay)
  9. 1932 — September 7: Minute Mysteries published (Austin Ripley; also known as Detectograms) — 71 mysteries
  10. 1933 — March: The Mystery Puzzle Book published (Wren, McKay)
  11. 1934 — November: Edward Powys Mathers, The Torquemada Puzzle Book (part later reprinted as Cain's Jawbone)
  12. 1935 — Photocrimes published in Britain in Weekly Illustrated
  13. 1936 — July 23: Murder Off Miami published in Britain (later reprinted in the U.S. as Crimefile No. 1)
  14. 1936 — September 1: Crimefile No. 1: File on Bolitho Blane (also known as Murder Off Miami)
  15. 1936 — September 20: Photocrimes in the Des Moines Register — the earliest U.S. example we have discovered so far
  16. 1936 — November 30: first Photocrimes book collection
  17. 1936 — The Marceau Case (dossier novel)
  18. 1936 — X. Jones of Scotland Yard (dossier novel)
  19. 1937 — Photocrimes puzzle-book edition under the same title (distinct from the 1936 collection; a 1944 edition may be a reprint)
  20. 1937 — May 1: Crimefile No. 2: File on Rufus Ray
  21. 1937 — August 1: File on Robert Prentice (originally the British Who Killed Robert Prentice?)
  22. 1937 — November 1: Crimefile No. 3: File on Fenton and Farr
  23. 1938 — January 1: Crimefile No. 4: File on Claudia Cragge
  24. 1938 — January 1: The Malinsay Massacre
  25. 1938 — Look magazine Photocrimes (22 cases)
  26. 1939 — July 1: Herewith the Clues
  27. 1940s — Photocrimes in the Des Moines Register, using work from David Nowinson (creator of "You Solve the Crime") and Harold Ripley (creator of Minute Mysteries)
